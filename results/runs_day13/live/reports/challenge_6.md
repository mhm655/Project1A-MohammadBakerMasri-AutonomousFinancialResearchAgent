# JPM, BAC, C, WFC, GS, MS: Company Profile

## Executive Summary

Citigroup Inc., Bank of America Corp., JPMorgan Chase & Co., Goldman Sachs Group Inc., and Wells Fargo & Co. are the major diversified banks in the United States, each operating multiple consumer, commercial and investment-banking segments[E10][E9][E8][E11][E7].  From FY2023 to FY2025 the three largest lenders (JPM, BAC and C) posted solid top-line growth of 6-8 % in FY2025, while Wells Fargo’s revenue continued to decline, falling 1.6 % in FY2019[E13][E14][E15]. *(unverified)*  Net margins improved for Citigroup (+1.1 ppt) and Bank of America (+1.5 ppt) in FY2025, reflecting a mix of pricing power and cost-control, whereas Wells Fargo’s margin contracted by 2.7 ppt, driven by higher credit costs and lower pricing power[E13][E14][E15]. *(unverified)*  **Analysis:** If the current pricing environment and cost-efficiency initiatives persist, the upward margin trends at C and BAC appear durable; however, Wells Fargo faces headwinds from a shrinking loan book and margin pressure, suggesting a more challenging near-term outlook.

**Margin trends**  

- Citigroup’s net margin rose from 15.7 % in FY 2024 to 16.8 % in FY 2025 (+1.1 ppt)[E13][E20]. *(unverified)*  
- Bank of America’s net margin rose from 25.5 % in FY 2024 to 27.0 % in FY 2025 (+1.5 ppt)[E14][E20]. *(unverified)*  
- Wells Fargo’s net margin fell from 25.9 % in FY 2018 to 23.2 % in FY 2019 (-2.7 ppt)[E15][E20]. *(unverified)*  

**Analysis:** The margin expansions at C and BAC likely stem from a combination of higher-interest-rate net interest margins (pricing) and disciplined expense management, as both firms reported strong revenue growth alongside modest cost increases in their filings.  Wells Fargo’s margin compression reflects a shrinking loan portfolio and higher credit-loss provisions, suggesting the trend may persist unless loan growth rebounds.
