# Tesla: Risk Assessment

## Executive Summary

Tesla, Inc. designs, manufactures, and sells electric vehicles as well as energy generation and storage systems globally [E4]. Financial results show declining revenue trajectories, with FY2025 (ended 31 December 2025) total revenue falling -2.93% to $94,827 million from $97,690 million in FY2024 (ended 31 December 2024), while operating income declined from $7,076 million in FY2024 to $4,355 million in FY2025 [E3, E5, E9]. Key takeaways highlight significant pressure on automotive and energy margins, ongoing regulatory scrutiny over Full Self-Driving (FSD) technologies, and intensifying competition from emerging and legacy auto manufacturers [E6, E7, E8].

## Operational Risks

Tesla faces substantial operational hazards regarding manufacturing expansion, key personnel concentration, and supply chain dependencies [E2]. As disclosed in its Form 10-K for FY2025 (ended 31 December 2025), Tesla is subject to risks related to scaling production at new factories, securing sufficient battery cell supplies, developing internal 4680 cell manufacturing, and ramping nascent initiatives such as Optimus and the Cybercab [E2, E6]. 

Operational key risks explicitly identified in SEC filings include:
- Heavy reliance on key personnel: The company is highly dependent on the services of Elon Musk, Technoking and CEO [E2, E4].
- Production delays and supply issues: Vulnerability to supplier component failures, quality issues, or cost spikes, alongside potential delays in factory construction timelines [E2].
- Product defects and recall risks: Risk of liability claims or operational disruptions if products contain defects or take longer than expected to achieve full self-driving functionality [E2].

Analysis: Executive key-person concentration in Elon Musk presents an operational continuity risk, while complex scaling requirements for new platforms (e.g., Terafab, Cybercab) expose Tesla to margin dilution during ramp-up phases [E2, E6, E7].

## Regulatory Risks

Tesla operates in a heavily regulated environment, with specific legal and compliance uncertainties tied to autonomous driving and trade policy [E2]. European regulators have kept 'Full Self-Driving' safety data secret amid ongoing scrutiny [E6]. Additionally, regulatory credit revenues remain key to automotive profitability, but benefits from tariffs and regulatory credits can be volatile—evidenced by more than $200 million in tariff benefits that expired between Q1 FY2026 and Q2 FY2026 [E7]. 

Primary regulatory risk factors from the Form 10-K for FY2025 (ended 31 December 2025) include [E2]:
- Unfavorable, political, tax, trade, or regulatory changes across international jurisdictions (including APAC and EMEA) [E2, E7].
- Evolving legal liability standards regarding product safety, product liability, and autonomous driving adoption [E2].

Analysis: Heightened regulatory scrutiny on driver-assistance systems could delay the global rollout of FSD features, restricting high-margin software revenue growth [E2, E6].
